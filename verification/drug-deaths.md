## What was open after the first check, and what closed it

### 1. "Scotland leaves substances unspecified in only ~2% of cases" — **VERIFIED**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Was** | Claimed in the pasted AI answer. No publisher behind it. Marked `CLAIMED — UNVERIFIED` on the page. |
| **Found** | *"In 2021, 25.1% of drug-related deaths registered in England and Wales had no information on the specific substances involved. The equivalent figure for Scotland was 1.9%, and for Northern Ireland the figure was 6.6%."* |
| **Source** | Source 3, section "The effect of death registration systems on drug-related death statistics", Figure 2. |
| **Verdict** | Confirmed, and understated — the gap is 25.1% vs 1.9%, not "~21% vs ~2%". |
| **Caveat that must travel with it** | This is **2021** data, and it is the most recent cross-UK comparison the three statistics producers have published together. It is not a 2024 figure and must not be presented as one. |

### 2. "Scotland's headline uses a different definition" — **VERIFIED, and it is the crux**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Was** | A question in the page's own hinge: *"Scotland's headline uses a different definition"*. Unanswered. |
| **Found** | NRS publishes **drug misuse** deaths — an ACMD (2000) definition, a *subset* of drug poisoning. ONS publishes **drug poisoning** deaths — the wide definition, including prescription and over-the-counter medicines. The guidance states drug poisoning is the measure that may be compared across the UK, and that **drug misuse "should not be used for comparisons between UK countries"**. |
| **Source** | Source 3, sections "Drug poisoning" and "Drug Misuse". Source 2, definitional note. |
| **Verdict** | Confirmed. Side-by-side figures — Scotland 1,017 (drug misuse, 2024) and England and Wales 5,565 (drug poisoning, 2024) — are **not** a valid ratio. |

### 3. "The claim itself (Scotland's drug deaths higher than England's)" — **VERIFIED on the comparable measure**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Found** | On **drug poisoning**, the comparable measure: *"The rate in Scotland has been consistently higher than the rest of the UK. In 2021 the rate in Scotland was more than double the rate in any other UK country."* |
| **Source** | Source 3, Figure 1 and its commentary. |
| **Verdict** | The claim is supported. What does not hold is the arithmetic usually shown to support it. This is the page's actual finding, and it is a better one than "the comparison is unfair". |

### 4. "Opioid deaths underestimated by roughly 50–55% (KCL / NPSUM)" — **PARTIALLY VERIFIED — the claim is inverted**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Was** | Not on the page; carried in the original research note as an unverified aside. Left off because it could not be traced. |
| **Source found** | Chen S, Mais D, Copeland CS, *Comparison of ONS and NPSUM data suggests that opioid-related deaths in England & Wales have been systematically underestimated*, International Journal of Drug Policy 145:104976, November 2025. Peer-reviewed, CC BY. `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugpo.2025.104976` |
| **What the paper's abstract says** | *"In 2011, 574 heroin/morphine-related deaths were identified by ONS; while the estimated number was 813 (95 % CIs 765–866), reflecting a 29 % underestimate. By 2022, the underestimate had extended to 36 % (1264 deaths identified by ONS; estimated number 1980 [95 % CIs 1894–2074])."* |
| **Where 54.7% comes from** | King's College London's institutional release for the paper: *"Opioid-related deaths from 2011 to 2022 are 54.7% higher than recorded… 39,232 compared to 25,364 deaths previously reported."* |
| **The error in the claim** | "54.7% higher than recorded" divides the gap by the **recorded** figure: (39,232 − 25,364) ÷ 25,364. "Underestimated by X%" divides the gap by the **true** figure: (39,232 − 25,364) ÷ 39,232 = **35.4%**. The claim takes the first number and attaches the second framing — turning a true statement into an overstatement of roughly twenty percentage points. |
| **Verdict** | The research is real. The sentence is wrong. Correct statements: *"the true number of opioid deaths is ~55% higher than the number recorded"*, or *"about 35% of opioid deaths went unrecorded"*. |
| **Confidence** | High on the source and its bibliographic record. Medium on the 54.7% and the 39,232 / 25,364 totals — those were verified in KCL's release, **not** in the paper's body text, which could not be retrieved in full. The 29% and 36% figures are quoted from the abstract itself. |
| **Now on the page** | Yes — as Beat 6, with the arithmetic shown both ways. It is the page's own subject occurring a second time, and the more instructive instance because the claim arrived attached to a real citation. |

### 5. Naming note

NPSUM is the **National Programme on Substance Use Mortality**, renamed from the National Programme on
Substance Abuse Deaths (NPSAD). Both names appear in circulation; the older one is not wrong, it is
out of date.

## Rules this audit follows

1. A figure is verified only by reading the publisher, not by a summary of the publisher.
2. A percentage is recorded with what it is a percentage **of**. "21.4% of deaths had no drug type" and
   "opioid deaths are undercounted by 50%" are not interchangeable, and a plausible reconstruction of
   one from the other is treated as fabrication.
3. Where a verified figure is older than the headline claim, the year travels in the sentence.
4. Anything unverified is either marked on the page or left off it. It is never quietly dropped, and
   never quietly promoted.
