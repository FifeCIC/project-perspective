PB-01 · Cascade · Forensic register

Two nations count the same death differently

The claim is that Scotland's drug deaths are higher than England's. This page is not about which nation is worse. It is about what changes when you can see how each number is made.

The claim as it is usually made

Scotland's drug deaths are far higher than England's — the worst in Europe.

As stated in political argument. The figure most often quoted is from the National Records of Scotland.

The strongest case for the claim · module M-15 · required

Why the comparison is not simply unfair

  • The ONS itself says its totals are usable across the UK. Its drug-poisoning measure has complete population coverage and can be compared with the other nations' figures.
  • The comparison is made per million people, not in raw counts. That is the correct method, and it already accounts for England being far larger.
  • If Scotland's rate really is higher, that difference is real — and a public-health emergency of that size is exactly the kind of thing a nation's politics should be arguing about.

Beat 2 · how the numbers are made

"The Office for National Statistics (ONS) does not have access to post-mortem reports or toxicology results, so the accuracy of figures depends on the information provided by the coroner on the death certificate."

In England and Wales, what gets recorded about a drug death is whatever the coroner writes on the certificate. The ONS does not see the toxicology.

ONS, section 5 and section 10 (Limitations), 2024 registrations. ✅ verified

Hinge 2 — answer to continue

Does knowing the two nations record deaths differently change how you read the comparison?

Beat 3 · the missing detail

In 2024, 1,192 deaths (21.4%) registered in England and Wales had no drug type recorded on the coroner's certificate. In those cases the record may say only "drug overdose" or "multiple drug toxicity".

The ONS is explicit about what that means: the totals are complete and reliable, but the breakdowns by drug are undercounts.

ONS, section 3 and section 5. ✅ verified

Which is the bigger problem?

Beat 4 · what the ONS says about comparing

"Our drug-poisoning statistics can be used for comparisons across the UK, as our measure offers complete population coverage. Figures for drug misuse and individual substances are not comparable because of differences in data collection methods and in the death registration system."

So the argument is narrower than it first appears. The totals can be compared. The breakdowns cannot — and a headline usually quotes a breakdown.

ONS, section 10 (Comparing with other statistics). ✅ verified

Beat 5 · the second reason the numbers are slippery

Only 37.2% of drug-poisoning deaths registered in 2024 actually occurred in 2024. The median delay between death and registration was 214 days in England and 279 days in Wales.

A figure labelled "2024" is mostly a figure about earlier years. That is a different problem from recording method — and the original argument ran the two together.

ONS, section 6 and section 7. ✅ verified
